Grana: Compositional Reward Modeling for Long-Horizon Robot Manipulation

Grana is a compositional reward function that measures how much of a long-horizon robot manipulation task is complete. It tracks progress lost after mistakes while preserving credit for completed steps. On real robots, this progress-based reward model supports policy learning through training-time reinforcement learning (RL) and action selection through test-time model predictive control (MPC).

01 · Modeling

Estimating whole-task progress from subtasks.

A failed insertion can erase the progress of the current attempt while leaving an earlier assembly step intact. Grana models these separately: it estimates progress within the unfinished subtask and combines it with credit for completed steps.

Compositional model architecture

Constructing progress supervision

We construct dense progress targets from sparse annotations of subtask boundaries, action-quality intervals, and subtask completion. Annotators mark suboptimal and regressed intervals; unmarked intervals are nominal. No frame-by-frame numerical progress scores are needed.

Progress supervision in this work comes from human annotations; our released codebase also supports GPT-assisted annotation for future use.

Nominal
Execution follows the task normally.Progress increases.
Suboptimal
Degraded execution without physical failure, such as a misaligned screw that remains held.Progress increases more slowly.
Regressed
Physical failure loses local progress, such as dropping the screw.Local progress resets to zero.

Then compose: whole-task target = (completed subtasks + local progress) / total subtasks. A local reset keeps credit for earlier steps that remain complete.

Target-construction details: Paper Sec. 3.2 and Appendix A.

02 · Reusable Knowledge

Learn reusable progress knowledge from shorter tasks.

Long-horizon tasks are harder to collect data for and difficult to anticipate in advance. We therefore learn reusable knowledge about task progress from diverse, mostly short manipulation tasks. This knowledge supports zero-shot prediction on unseen tasks and efficient adaptation to longer tasks with limited demonstrations.

We pretrain the reward model on 200 tasks with subtask descriptions and action-quality annotations, and evaluate zero-shot predictions on 50 held-out tasks. The long assembly tasks and both offline-RL tasks are excluded from pretraining.

03 · Evaluation

Reward model evaluation.

We test whether the progress knowledge learned during pretraining transfers to unseen tasks, longer procedures, and a different robot. We also examine the contribution of composition, the cost of inference, and how predictions respond when execution fails.

04.1 · Application

Train-time RL.

Grana turns changes in predicted progress into learning signals for real robot policies. Its zero-shot rewards improve offline RL on Star insertion and Tube transfer; task-adapted rewards also improve long-horizon assembly through progress-weighted behavioral cloning.

Across four reward sources, lower held-out RMSE corresponds to higher or tied policy success on both offline-RL tasks.

04.2 · Application · Test-time MPC

Improve execution without updating the policy.

A policy can get stuck or move on before finishing a step. Grana uses progress estimates to decide when planning is needed and to select among imagined futures. On tractor assembly, Grana-guided MPC improves success from 2/10 to 9/10 across ten initial layouts, without updating the policy.

Show action selection and tractor results · Appendix E

Grana decides when to switch MPC on and off. While MPC is on, it scores batches of four futures predicted by GR00T-2. When predicted progress trends downward for all four candidates, it rejects the batch and requests new futures.

From an accepted batch, the robot executes the candidate with the highest final predicted whole-task progress, composed from the selected subtask and its local progress. It then replans from the new observation.

In tractor assembly, GR00T-2 samples four action chunks per round, each with a predicted 1.6 s video. A Grana variant with a Qwen3.5-0.8B backbone, adapted to this task, scores the futures. With the same GR00T-2 policy run from 10 different initial layouts, success rises from 2/10 without MPC to 9/10 with MPC, without any policy update. Paper Appendix E and Fig. 9.
